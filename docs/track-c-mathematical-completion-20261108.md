## Mathematical theorem and finite bound

Let K consist of finite two-sorted Boolean rings in the non-unital
language (zero, symmetric difference, intersection), equipped with
an F₂ pairing additive in both arguments. Both sorts may be zero
rings. If K is a countable Fraïssé class, its countable
ultrahomogeneous limit is ω-categorical.

For nL left and nR right generators, set m=2^nL-1, k=2^nR-1.
The number of genuine automorphism orbits on ordered tuple pairs
is at most

    N(nL,nR) = sum_{s=0}^m sum_{t=0}^k C(m,s) C(k,t) 2^(s*t).

For total tagged tuple length n, the bound is
sum_{j=0}^n C(n,j) N(j,n-j): 1, 4, 26, 466 for n=0,1,2,3.

## Independent mathematical proof routes (not independent agents)

**Route A: disjoint positive membership fibres.**
For an n-tuple x, each ambient atom has a membership pattern
p in {0,1}^n. The atoms with p=0 are outside the union u of
the generators and are never part of the non-unital generated ring.
For every p≠0, let c_p be the union of atoms of pattern p.
Choose i0∈p and express c_p as x_i0 intersected with
the remaining positive generators and the relative complements
(u minus x_j) for negative coordinates. Every such c_p is a
non-unital ring term. The nonzero c_p are exactly the intrinsic
atoms of the generated ring, since each generator is their union
and all unions of disjoint c_p are ring terms. Hence there are
2^{|P|} generated elements and at most 2^n-1 intrinsic atoms.

For two sorts, define M_pq = beta(c_p,d_q) for the realised
positive patterns P_L,P_R. Every generated element is a union of
some c_p or d_q, and additivity gives its pairing by summing
M_pq over the selected patterns. Thus the triple (P_L,P_R,M)
uniquely determines the pointed generated two-sort ring-pairing
structure, preserving every generator and original pairing value.
Conversely a pointed generated-isomorphism preserves the intrinsic
atoms, their membership patterns and this pairing matrix.

**Route B: unit-free Boolean polynomial functions.**
The free non-unital Boolean ring on n generators consists
of F₂-polynomial functions on {0,1}^n vanishing at the zero
pattern; their algebraic normal forms have no constant term.
For each nonzero pattern p, its indicator polynomial starts
with some positive generator x_i0, so expanding all other
positive/negative membership factors has no constant term.
The image of evaluation on the realised positive patterns P is
therefore all F₂^P. Its standard basis consists exactly of the
intrinsic atoms c_p of Route A. The cross-pairing is an
F₂-bilinear form with matrix M in these two indicator bases.
This independently reconstructs the same pointed finite type.

## Homogeneity and actual global orbits

The standard Fraïssé limit is a countable increasing union of finite
K-structures and is ultrahomogeneous. The substructure generated by
a finite tuple in the non-unital language is finite, with its *own*
intrinsic identity. The code above is independent of the finite
stage and atom enumeration by taking a common finite refinement
and using partial-owner map naturality.

Equal profiles produce an isomorphism of these finite generated
substructures. Ultrahomogeneity extends that isomorphism to a
global automorphism of the original two-sorted pairing.
Conversely global automorphisms preserve profiles. Therefore
actual global orbits are exactly the realised profiles and there
are finitely many for each tuple length. The standard
Ryll–Nardzewski theorem then gives omega-categoricity.

## Adversarial counterexamples and edge cases

* The empty tuple generates {0}, not the ambient unit.
* Either sort can be a zero ring and the corresponding matrix empty.
* Zero-pattern ambient atoms and their multiplicities are discarded.
* Omitting the pairing matrix is false: [0] and [1] yield
  different pointed two-sort structures even with the same patterns.
* Homogeneity is essential. In a three-atom finite Boolean ring,
  a singleton and a two-atom union have the same non-unital
  one-generator profile but cannot be exchanged by an ambient
  automorphism, which preserves atom support cardinality.
* RingEquiv notation is valid on non-unital rings, but closure must
  be NonUnitalSubring.closure, never ambient unital closure.
* Local finiteness needs no injectivity to be bounded by a finite
  image; the equivalence onto the generated image needs injectivity.
