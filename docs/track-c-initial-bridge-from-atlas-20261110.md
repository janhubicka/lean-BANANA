### Proven mathematically, proposed in Lean

Let M be a two-sorted non-unital ring pairing with a directed finite
atom-stage atlas, and let p and q be finite pairs of tuples in M.
Suppose that e is an actual isomorphism from the two non-unital
subrings generated by p to those generated by q, preserving
the pairing on every pair of generated elements and sending the
distinguished generators of p to those of q.

The atlas provides a finite stage containing p. The two generated
subrings S_L and S_R are finite because they lie in the image
of the finite stage's ring homomorphisms. The stage-to-ambient
generated ring isomorphisms give S_L and S_R intrinsic Boolean-ring
structures. Their multiplicative units are NOT assumed to be
units of M and may differ from a unit in any finite ambient stage.

Restrict the original pairing to S_L x S_R. It is additive in both
coordinates by the finite-stage atlas pairing theorem. Thus it
defines a finite native Folkman object P. The merged Track B
finite-code theorem provides an isomorphism
I : P -> folkmanNativeCodeRealisation C
for C = folkmanNativeFinCode P.pairing.

There are now TWO faithful embeddings of P into M:
* f_L, f_R are the original subtype inclusions S_L -> M_L
  and S_R -> M_R;
* g_L, g_R are the compositions of the isomorphism e with
  the original inclusions of the generated subrings of q.

Both pairs are injective non-unital ring homomorphisms.
The first preserves the original pairing by definition of P.
The second preserves it because e preserves every pairing value,
not merely the values on the distinguished generators.

Precompose f and g with the inverse of I to obtain two pairs
of embeddings of the *same* finite atom code C into M.
They are injective, preserve zero, addition and multiplication,
and each reconstructs exactly the same matrix C.edge on all
finite atom subsets. This is a finite paired bridge B.
The code coordinates of the named generators are I(p_i).
The first embedding sends them to p_i, and the second sends
them to q_i, by the generator-preservation fields of e.

Consequently **the finite initial paired bridge requirement follows
from the directed stage atlas** and the existing finite native
representation theorem. It is not another independent infinite
assumption.

The two new source modules are:
- FolkmanNativeInitialPairedBridge.lean: construct any paired
  bridge from two specified pairing-preserving native embeddings,
  and prove the application equations for the four maps.
- FolkmanInitialBridgeFromAtlas.lean: instantiate those maps on
  actual finite generated substructures, giving
  folkmanHasInitialPairedBridges_of_atlas. This reduces genuine
  generated homogeneity and two-sort/mixed-sort finite orbits to
  the atlas and the paired-bridge one-point extension property.

### Remaining construction: EPP implies four one-point extensions

There is a further mathematical reduction, NOT YET FORMALISED in Lean.

Assume M is locally finite in the original non-unital language and
has the finite embedding-extension property (EPP):

For any finite objects A,B of the Folkman class, any embedding
i:A -> B, and any embedding h:A -> M, there is an embedding
H:B -> M with H composed with i equal to h.

Given a finite paired bridge B0, and an ambient left element x
to be included in its SOURCE image, form the non-unital finite
substructure B1 generated by the entire source image of B0
together with x. Local finiteness ensures B1 is finite, and its
intrinsic Boolean-ring units make it a valid finite Folkman object.
The source copy of B0 embeds in B1 by an actual original-language
embedding i. Applying EPP to i and the TARGET copy of B0
extends that target embedding to B1. The identity inclusion of
B1 into M and the extended target embedding give the required
larger paired bridge, with x in its source-left image.

For a source-right element use the other generated sort.
For a target-left or target-right element, exchange the roles
of the two copies. Thus EPP plus local finiteness proves all
four one-point paired bridge extension properties.

This argument uses the FULL cross-sort pairing on B1 and on the
extension maps, not only the underlying two Boolean-ring maps.
It assumes no global identity in M. It also requires that the
enlarged source substructure contain the complete original image
of B0 on both sorts; including only the named tuple generators
would not suffice.

What remains mathematically is the standard fair Fraïssé
construction of a countable locally finite M with EPP, from
Track B's native finite HP/JEP/AP/countability certificate.
The pinned Mathlib Fraisse module still marks general existence
as an open implementation TODO. Existing Track C finite profile
and cofinal-bridge theorems would then prove genuine global
automorphism-orbit finiteness and the standard externally cited
Ryll–Nardzewski conclusion.

### Adversarial tests

A separate Python finite semantic checker was run locally from
the source file
/mnt/data/folkman_initial_bridge_semantic_check_20261110.py.

It sampled ambient rings up to four atoms on each sort:
2,740 cases with matching realised positive membership signatures;
2,602 cases also had identical complete fibre-pairing matrices.
All 4,548 complete generated-pairing comparisons passed in those
valid cases. Of them, 2,485 had a zero-generated sort, and 676
had different multiplicities of corresponding ambient fibres.
There was one case where named-generator pairing values agreed
but the full fibre matrix disagreed, confirming that the
weaker pairing check cannot replace the complete finite profile.

A three-atom finite Boolean ring with zero cross-pairing has
elements {0} and {0,1} with isomorphic one-generated non-unital
subrings, hence an initial paired bridge, but no ambient
automorphism taking one to the other. Thus the initial-bridge
lemma does NOT silently discharge EPP or generated homogeneity.
