# Mathematical representations and statement fidelity

A formalisation of a class of finite structures must specify the objects, embeddings, actual copies and composition. A choice of finite coordinates is harmless only when the correspondence transports the embeddings and image copies correctly.

## Boolean–BANANA

A finite object is a nontrivial Boolean algebra with a parity map into the two-element field, additive on disjoint elements and zero on the greatest element. An atom has mark zero or one, and the sum of all atom marks is zero. An embedding is unital and preserves the parity map. In atom coordinates an embedding is described by a partition of the target atoms into nonempty fibres over the source atoms with the correct parity sums.

The [BooleanBanana entry point](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BooleanBanana.lean#L21) names the native finite objects, embeddings and the explicit countable cylinder limit. [Native copy transport](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BooleanBananaCopyTransport.lean) concerns genuine image copies, not embeddings with named atoms.

## Folkman–BANANA

Objects consist of two finite Boolean rings with a biadditive cross-pairing into the two-element field. The language does not name the greatest element. Mathlib gives a finite Boolean ring its intrinsic unit; this is not a requirement that an embedding preserve the ambient unit. The embeddings use non-unital ring homomorphisms on both sorts. The zero ring is allowed.

Atom fibres now need not cover the target atoms. The remaining atoms are essential for the strong-amalgamation argument. The [finite Fraïssé-class theorem](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/FolkmanNativeCompleteFiniteClass.lean#L85) supplies hereditary closure, joint embedding, strong amalgamation, a countable family of native isomorphism representatives and unbounded finite members. The hereditary proof gives each finite subring its own unit, namely the join of its elements. [Sortwise intersection](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/FolkmanNativeSortwiseIntersection.lean) verifies exact strong intersection separately on both sorts.

For model theory, the library uses a first-order Cartesian-product encoding. The projection operations encode both ring coordinates. [Native embedding correspondence](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/FolkmanFirstOrder.lean), [substructure correspondence](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/FolkmanFirstOrderSubstructure.lean) and [limit decoding](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/FolkmanFirstOrderDecodingLimit.lean) justify passage between this encoding and the two rings. The encoded first-order carrier is not literally the disjoint union of the native sorts. Finite mixed-sort orbit statements and the native nonamenability theorem use the decoded structures.

## BANANA

An object is a pair of finite-dimensional vector spaces with a bilinear pairing into the two-element field, which may be degenerate. In coordinates, it is a rectangular matrix. Embeddings are pairs of injective linear maps preserving the pairing. A copy is a pair of image subspaces, not a pair of ordered bases. [Native copy system](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaNativeCopySystem.lean) defines composition, images and transport of the copy degree. [Native coherent EPPA](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaNativeCoherentEppa.lean#L161) works for arbitrary native finite carriers, not only fixed matrix bases.

The tagged relational presentation used for model theory records the two sorts and their operations by relations. Its induced relational substructures need not be closed under the native operations. This is why one must not automatically identify its relational age with the functional age. [Functional presentation](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaFunctionalLimit.lean) encodes addition and both zero constants. [Tagged model decoding](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaTaggedModelDecoding.lean) and [sorted syntax](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaSortedSyntax.lean) explain the first-order model-theoretic correspondence.

## Alternating spaces

The native carrier is a finite vector space with an alternating bilinear form, possibly degenerate. The actual embeddings are injective linear isometries. [Alternating-space copy system](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/SymplecticCopySystem.lean) uses image subspaces and derives AP from the countable symplectic model. No nondegeneracy hypothesis is imposed on the finite members of the age.

## Copies, embeddings and expanded generated domains

If A is a finite source, its embeddings onto any one copy form a torsor for Aut(A). A finite copy Ramsey degree therefore corresponds to the embedding degree multiplied by the order of Aut(A); infinitude transfers in both directions. See [the first-order multiplier](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/FirstOrderEmbeddingCopyDegree.lean#L108) and [native classifications](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/RamseyDegreeClassifications.lean#L15).

For a hereditary expansion class, an expansion type of A is an isomorphism type of a pair consisting of the substructure generated by an A-copy in the expanded language and that distinguished copy. Added functions or constants may enlarge this domain. Finiteness of types on the unchanged reduct carrier alone would not be the relevant hypothesis. The [first-order generated-expansion theorem](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/FirstOrderGeneratedRamseyExpansion.lean#L112) counts precisely these pointed types.

The [native pointed-cover interface](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/NativePointedRamseyExpansion.lean#L24) does not require a particular first-order syntax. Let S be the reduct copy system and T the expanded copy system. A reduct map sends expanded objects and embeddings to their native presentations and preserves composition. Every reduct object embeds into the reduct of an expanded object. The expanded system has the embedding Ramsey property. For a fixed native source, choose a finite list of expanded objects with distinguished native copies; every copy in an expanded target must be the image of one of these pointed objects under an expanded embedding.

For an ordinary hereditary expansion class, choose a small family of representatives. Conjugate reduct embeddings by the chosen isomorphisms to the native presentations; the middle isomorphisms cancel in a composite. Every copy generates an expanded substructure, which belongs to the expanded age by heredity. A pointed isomorphism from its representative followed by inclusion gives the cover. This is valid even if the generating copy is not itself an expanded substructure. The expanded Ramsey property supplies the remaining field. The [native bound](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/NativePointedRamseyExpansion.lean#L72) applies, and its [three obstruction theorems](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/NativePointedRamseyExpansion.lean#L119) contradict the native infinite degrees.

This last passage from arbitrary many-sorted language syntax to the abstract interface is a mathematical correspondence argument, not a separate formal syntax translation. Relative validation markers distinguish it from a literal first-order theorem.
