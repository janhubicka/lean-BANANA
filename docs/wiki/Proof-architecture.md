# Mathematical proof architecture

This is a human-readable account of the formalisation, not a trace of Lean tactics. The [statement crosswalk](Formalised-statements.md) points to the checked endpoints; the [proof map](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/README.md#proof-coverage) also lists the auxiliary modules.

## 1. Finite classes and homogeneity

The Boolean constructions reduce embeddings to nonempty atom fibres with prescribed parity. Strong amalgamation joins two fibre grids, choosing the target marks so that both prescribed parity margins hold. The Folkman construction keeps private atoms outside the image of the common base unit, ensuring that non-unital maps remain injective and that the two images meet exactly on the base. For its cross-pairing, one extends the compatible pairings from the common vector subspaces, assigning the remaining mixed complementary values freely. This gives the native finite-class theorem.

For BANANA, the analogous split-vector-space construction directly amalgamates arbitrary pairings. Completing a finite pairing to a perfect pairing allows one to use linear extension and the contragredient action on the other sort. Explicit countable models, finite age and homogeneity, and the finite orbit or first-order interfaces give the limit results. The Folkman encoded countable limit is constructed using the general finite Fraïssé limit machinery; its native rings are recovered from finite tuple factorisations.

## 2. Coherent EPPA and dynamics of BANANA

Every finite pairing embeds in a perfect pairing of side dimension equal to the sum of its two dimensions. For each isomorphism type of subpair choose a representative, align its perfect completion inside the common witness, and extend the representative's automorphism group by the identity on an orthogonal complement. Choosing transport isomorphisms from the representatives then gives extensions for all partial isomorphisms. For composable partial isomorphisms the intermediate transport cancels; this proves coherence. The native endpoint [exists_coherentEppaWitness](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaNativeCoherentEppa.lean#L161) expresses the identity and composition laws on actual subpairs.

The native countable group has an increasing sequence of finite general linear groups with dense union in the pointwise topology. A general invariant-measure argument for dense directed unions of finite groups supplies amenability. For ample generics, perfect-total finite systems give cofinal extensions and amalgamation. Dense open extension requirements and a back-and-forth argument yield one comeagre diagonal conjugacy orbit in each finite power.

Key sources: [Finite-group amenability](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/FiniteSubgroupAmenability.lean), [Native amenability](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaLimitTopologicalAmenability.lean), [Finite-system JEP/WAP](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaFiniteSystemsJepWap.lean), [Ample generics](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaLimitGenericBackAndForth.lean#L285).

## 3. Persistent colourings

In Boolean–BANANA, a copy of the two-marked-atom source is a partition into two odd-marked blocks. Choosing an ambient atom makes it possible to distinguish one block without changing the language. Colour by its marked-atom count modulo a power of two. The odd subset-sum lemma forces every colour in each all-marked target of the prescribed size.

In Folkman–BANANA the colour is an integer count of pairs of atoms on the two sides whose pairing is one. Its parity is the native pairing. In a diagonal target the relevant summands are odd; the subset-sum argument again forces all odd residues. The private atoms of a non-unital embedding do not enter the chosen joins.

For BANANA, choose a perfect completion and identify coordinate vectors with subsets. Colour the two one-dimensional sources by the cardinality of the coordinate intersection modulo q, where q is a power of two. The parity is the bilinear pairing value. The residue lemma works on affine functions in dimension q−1, subject to the mixed matrix identity. Expand the residue-generating product in the truncated algebra over the two-element field with t = 1+z and t^q = 0. Terms of degree below q−1 have even solution counts; terms above q−1 vanish. At the remaining degree, Cauchy–Binet gives determinant one. Hence every residue occurs an odd number of times. Affine slices ensure the nonzero-vector requirements. The checked colouring theorem is on actual image copies.

The alternating-space application uses a quadratic refinement whose polar form is the alternating form. A quadratic residue-fibre parity theorem supplies the line-colouring obstruction, which propagates to all positive-dimensional finite sources using amalgamation.

## 4. Degree propagation, exact values and expansions

If a source P embeds into A, amalgamate a copy of A over each P-copy in the desired target. Colour A-copies by the set of colours occurring on their P-copies. A finite A-degree gives a finite P-degree. Thus an infinite-degree smaller source forces infinite degree for any larger source containing it. The general lemma is in [lean-successors](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-successors/blob/d9be105f73ca5a0f3f411b87d4c2419d0eb94753/SuccessorTree/CopyRamsey.lean#L257).

Every BANANA object with both sorts positive contains one of the two line-pair sources. If one sort is zero, binary Graham–Leeb–Rothschild, proved via the successor-tree library, yields copy degree one. Uniform embedding fibres give the multiplier by the source automorphism group. The [classification](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/RamseyDegreeClassifications.lean#L15) combines these results.

For expansions, a finite family of pointed generated expanded objects covers native copies. Apply the embedding Ramsey property successively to this finite list and pull back each native copy colouring along expanded embeddings. Within a target image, each representative contributes at most one colour. This proves the [pointed-cover bound](https://github.com/janhubicka/lean-BANANA/blob/4fabdd7cf1bf1206912cd0d6c92e685b04e769cf/BANANA/NonPrecompact/NativePointedRamseyExpansion.lean#L72) and the obstruction from infinite copy degree. This proof does not need reasonableness of the expansion beyond the stated lift and cover assumptions.

## 5. Dynamical consequences and Boolean charge flows

The finite-degree fixed-point argument uses an extremely amenable subgroup and a finite cover by its orbits. Lean also formalises the route via metrisability of the universal minimal flow and finite degrees. The native BANANA group has infinite degrees, hence no such co-precompact subgroup. A native Stone–Čech orbit construction gives nonmetrisability of the universal minimal flow. The model-theoretic development uses canonical zero-base amalgamation and finite-parameter colourings to obtain DEERP, with the appropriate tagged and sorted translations.

For Boolean–BANANA, the space of finitely additive two-adic charges with the prescribed parities and total zero is compact and metrisable. Finite residue realisation and homogeneity give dense orbits. For a fixed nontrivial Boolean element, each orbit has only countably many possible values, while every value fibre is nowhere dense. Thus every orbit is meagre.

The positive-mass development realises each nonempty set of odd primes on the same marked limit using an exhaustive uniform tower. Dense finite permutation stages give amenability of the mass-preserving subgroup. Uniform partitions force its invariant Stone probability to be unique. An amenable supergroup must preserve that probability, yielding maximality; the clopen mass values detect the allowed primes and hence distinguish conjugacy classes. The concrete cardinality theorem gives exactly continuum many conjugacy classes of closed maximal amenable subgroups.
