## Countable exhaustive finite chain

The signature has two sorts, constant zero on each sort, non-unital
ring addition and multiplication, and a cross-sort relation E
encoding the F₂ pairing. Finite substructures are closed under
zero, addition and multiplication, not an ambient unit. The finite
non-unital subrings have their own intrinsic multiplicative
identities, obtained by finite joins of their elements. Zero rings
are permitted. The pair of zero rings embeds uniquely and
injectively into every class member, giving the common base for JEP.

There are countably many finite class objects and finite embeddings
up to isomorphism. Start from the two zero rings and construct an
increasing chain A₀→A₁→... of finite Folkman structures. Dovetail
all *actual extension requirements*: a finite embedding f:B→C and
an embedding e:B→A_n to a stage already constructed. To satisfy
one at current stage A_k (k≥n), amalgamate A_k with C over B,
using (A_n→A_k)∘e and f. The resulting D is A_{k+1}, and its
two embeddings provide the commuting extension witness.
Preserve all previous stages through the A_k→D transition.
Every enabled requirement is eventually met.

This gives an **embedding extension property** for the direct
limit M: any B→M factors through some finite stage because B is
finite, and the enumerated requirement extends it along every
finite embedding B→C. Taking B to be the two zero rings shows
that every finite Folkman object embeds into M. Hence age(M)=K.

Construct M by taking the direct limit of the finite underlying
sets in each sort, identifying two representatives when their
images agree in a common later stage. Each transition is injective
and preserves all operations and the full pairing. Thus those
operations and β are well-defined on the countable limit.
There need not be a greatest element or an ambient unit.

Every finite tuple lies in a finite stage and therefore generates
a finite non-unital subring on each sort. Two finite presentations
of the same tuple have a common finite refinement in the limit;
the corresponding compatible partial owners reproduce the
original maps on **all** ring elements. This supplies Track C's
directed finite-stage atlas and its choice-independent profiles.

## Explicit generated-structure back-and-forth

Given any isomorphism f₀:S→T between **finite generated**
two-sort substructures of M, enumerate all elements of both
sorts in a single tagged countable list. Maintain finite
substructures S_k,T_k and isomorphisms f_k:S_k→T_k.

At each even step choose the first element a outside S_k,
let C be the finite substructure generated by S_k together
with a, and apply the embedding extension property to
S_k↪C and the embedding f_k:S_k→M. This extends f_k to
an embedding g:C→M. Set S_{k+1}=C and T_{k+1}=g(C).
At odd steps apply the same argument to f_k^{-1}, forcing
the next element into the range. Each extension preserves
zero, addition, multiplication and the pairing in the
exact original non-unital language.

The union of the finite isomorphisms is a total bijection on
both sorts. Every finite operation/pairing check is contained
in one finite stage, so the union and its inverse preserve
all symbols. Hence the union is a genuine global pairing
automorphism extending f₀: exactly the Track C
FolkmanGeneratedHomogeneous property.

## Orbit bounds and omega-categoricity

Track C's complete positive-pattern/pairing-matrix profile
classifies finitely generated pointed two-sort substructures.
The extension theorem above makes equality of profiles
equivalent to genuine global automorphism conjugacy; global
automorphisms preserve profiles independently of homogeneity.
For n_L,n_R tuples, if m=2^{n_L}-1 and k=2^{n_R}-1 the
number of possible profiles is bounded by

   N(n_L,n_R)=Σ_{s=0}^m Σ_{t=0}^k C(m,s)C(k,t)2^{st}.

For tagged tuples of length n, sum C(n,j)N(j,n-j) over
j=0,...,n. This is finite, proving oligomorphicity; the
standard Ryll–Nardzewski theorem gives ω-categoricity.

## Adversarial failure modes

- Schedule actual embeddings e:B→A_n, **not** only object types;
  otherwise the embedding extension property is not guaranteed.
- AP gives JEP here because both zero rings form a valid base.
- Ensure all transitions preserve the **full original** β
  matrix on all elements, not merely a surrogate atom code.
- Alternating back-and-forth must exhaust *both* sorts and
  both domain and range, using generated substructures rather
  than individual coordinate maps in a functional language.
- Finite non-unital generated rings may omit the ambient unit.
- Equal finite positive profiles do not imply ambient conjugacy
  in an arbitrary nonhomogeneous structure: in a three-atom
  Boolean ring a singleton and a two-atom union have equal
  one-generator profiles but are in different ambient orbits.
