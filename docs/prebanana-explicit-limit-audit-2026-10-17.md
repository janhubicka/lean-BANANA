## Key lemma: finite marked embeddings factor through one stage

Let `M` denote the cylinder algebra. For a finite marked Boolean
algebra `A`, suppose `e : A → M` is an injective unital Boolean map
preserving the F₂ mark.

1. Each of the finitely many elements `e(a)` is represented by a
   subset of words at a finite depth `n_a`.
2. Choose `N = max_a n_a` and transport all word subsets to depth
   `N` by the genuine tower embeddings. Their cylinder sets and marks
   are unchanged.
3. Let `g(a)` be the transported word subset. The stage-`N` cylinder
   map is injective. Since `e` preserves `0,1,∨,∧`, their equations
   pull back along this injective cylinder map, giving a *bounded-lattice
   homomorphism* `g:A→B_N`.
4. Injectivity of `g` follows from injectivity of `e`; preservation
   of marking follows from the stage-independent cylinder mark.
   Consequently `g` is a genuine finite marked embedding and
   `e(a)=i_N(g(a))` for every `a`.

This is exactly the scope of draft PR #248, building on draft PR #247.
It is important to factor **all Boolean elements**, not only atoms,
and to avoid silently replacing the countable algebra by a finite one.

## Extension property of M

Take finite marked `A,B`, a marked embedding `f:A→B`, and a marked
embedding `e:A→M`. Factor `e` through a finite stage:
`e=i_n∘g`, with `g:A→B_n`. Apply the verified arbitrary finite
extension theorem to obtain `h:B→B_{n+k}` with
`h∘f=i_{n,n+k}∘g`. Then
`j=i_{n+k}∘h:B→M` is a marked embedding and
`j∘f=e`, because cylinder embeddings commute with stage refinement.

No homogeneity hypothesis is used in this deduction. The result is
stronger than mere joint embedding or universality.

## Finite age and local finiteness

Every finite marked structure embeds into M by finite-stage
universality and the cylinder embedding. Conversely, a finite
substructure of M is a finite Boolean subalgebra with the inherited
disjoint-additive marking; it is thus a structure in the pre-BANANA
class. A finite tuple of M is represented simultaneously in some
finite stage; the Boolean subalgebra generated by that tuple is
finite, so M is locally finite. The carrier is countable and
infinite, since stages with arbitrarily many atoms embed into M.

## Ultrahomogeneity and omega-categoricity

Given a marked isomorphism between finite Boolean subalgebras of M,
alternate forth and back through enumerations of the two copies of
M. At each step, adjoin the next element, take the finite Boolean
subalgebra it generates with the current domain/range, and apply the
extension property to extend the partial marked isomorphism.
The union of this chain is a marking-preserving Boolean automorphism
of M extending the original partial isomorphism.

For an r-tuple, the atoms of the generated Boolean algebra correspond
to realised membership patterns in `{0,1}^r`. The `F₂` mark of each
pattern block adds one of finitely many labels. Equal complete
profiles determine a marking-preserving isomorphism between the
generated subalgebras; ultrahomogeneity extends it to an
automorphism of M. Therefore M has finitely many orbits on r-tuples.
The deduction of ω-categoricity uses the standard
Ryll–Nardzewski theorem and should be stated with its citation,
not mislabelled as an in-Lean consequence unless explicitly formalised.

## Adversarial checks to apply before validation markers

* **Different depths:** equal cylinders at different levels need
  equal marking *after transport to a common level*; neither
  same-level cylinder injectivity nor global parity alone is enough.
* **Correct language:** embeddings must preserve both Boolean
  constants/operations and the unary F₂ marking; a surjective
  atom-owner partition alone is not an embedding.
* **Finite support:** the factorisation lemma must quantify over
  every element of a finite subalgebra, including Boolean
  combinations of a given tuple.
* **Nontriviality:** the empty Boolean ring is not allowed in this
  unital class; the two-element zero-marked Boolean algebra is the
  correct minimal structure.
* **Quantifier order:** the extension theorem must hold for every
  chosen finite diagram `A→B` and `A→M` before one picks
  the later stage. A common ambient stage not respecting the base
  map does not suffice.
* **Back-and-forth:** checking only the 'forth' direction produces
  an embedding, not a surjective automorphism. Both directions
  and marking preservation at the union are necessary.
* **Topological separation:** constructing M and classifying finite
  tuple orbits does not itself construct a Stone action, invariant
  measure or prove non-amenability. Those remain independent
  proof obligations.
