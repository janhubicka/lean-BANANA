# Classical dependencies of the BANANA circulation draft

This audit covers the classical results used in the circulation proofs at
manuscript commit [7ef8d62](https://github.com/janhubicka/BANANA/commit/7ef8d62c63f5a5348b625e386d26d5a1f1439e4e).
The local checks used Lean 4.35.0-rc3 and mathlib
[5bd58ac](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/5bd58ac291422a21f412ae354c91e7d172255a2c).

## Model theory and Ramsey theory

| Result needed | Coverage and next use |
| --- | --- |
| Fraïssé existence and uniqueness for the finite classes | Mathlib's Fraïssé API provides age, hereditary property, joint embedding, ordinary amalgamation, back-and-forth and uniqueness. Its countable age-realisation result does not itself give homogeneity. Cameron Freer's existence theorem was compiled from four unmodified files on BANANA's mathlib pin. Its axiom report lists only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. It takes an IsFraisse class in a language with countably many function symbols and returns a countable Fraïssé limit. The first-order Folkman product encoding uses coordinate projections, ring operations, zero and the pairing. Lean proves that embeddings between encoded structures are exactly the native non-unital pairing-preserving maps. Lean transfers the finite class certificate to mathlib's first-order interface. The external theorem then gives the encoded countable limit in a separately checked instantiation. Lean recovers the native non-unital rings and pairing from finite tuple factorisations. The concrete encoded-limit factorisation and finite native mixed-sort tuple-orbit bounds are checked, conditional on the countable Fraïssé limit. Lean instantiates the forward implication on the marked Boolean cylinder, the tagged relational presentation of the native BANANA limit and the encoded Folkman–BANANA Fraïssé limit. To reproduce the external Folkman instantiation, add the compiled modules
from the pinned Freer revision above to the Lean search path and check:

```lean
import BANANA.NonPrecompact.FolkmanFirstOrderFraisse
import ComputableModelTheory.ModelTheory.FraisseExistence

open FirstOrder CategoryTheory SuccessorTree.NonPrecompact

theorem exists_folkmanFirstOrderLimit :
    ∃ (M : Bundled.{0} folkmanLanguage.Structure) (_ : Countable M),
      Language.IsFraisseLimit folkmanFirstOrderClass M := by
  letI := folkmanFirstOrderClass_isFraisse
  obtain ⟨M, hM, hF⟩ := Language.exists_isFraisseLimit_of_isFraisse folkmanFirstOrderClass
  exact ⟨M, hM, hF⟩
```

The four required modules are `ExtensionRichFamily`,
`ExtensionRichDirectLimit`, `RepresentativeAge` and `FraisseExistence`.
The checked instantiation depends only on the three standard Lean axioms
listed in the audit. The BANANA build uses its existing mathlib pin.

## Topological dynamics and measures

| Result needed | Coverage and next use |
| --- | --- |
| Kechris–Rosendal Theorem 6.2 | The direction needed is that JEP and WAP for finite systems of partial automorphisms yield a comeagre diagonal conjugacy orbit on each finite power of the automorphism group. The finite systems are checked in Lean. Lean derives a dense diagonal conjugacy orbit in every finite power of the native group from JEP and Baire category. It also checks that the tuples with dense orbit form a comeagre set. The conclusion that one orbit is comeagre in each power still uses the cited criterion. [Native dense conjugacy](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaLimitDenseConjugacy.lean) [Finite-system verification](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaFiniteSystemsJepWap.lean) |
| Ben Yaacov–Melleray–Tsankov and Zucker | Lemma EA-degree uses the proof of BMT Corollary 3.3 for metrisability from a closed co-precompact extremely amenable subgroup of a Polish group, then Zucker Theorem 8.7 for finite embedding degrees when the universal minimal flow is metrisable. Finite embedding degrees bound copy degrees. The nonmetrisability proof also uses BMT Theorem 1.1 in the reverse direction. No matching formalisation was found. Lean proves the degree bound directly for locally finite ultrahomogeneous first-order structures. The continuous colouring action gives a finite palette, co-precompactness gives finite orbit representatives, and compactness produces a finite family of translations whose full target images lie in one finite ambient substructure. The manuscript uses BMT and Zucker for the degree bound. The direct Lean proof gives that bound and instantiates it on native BANANA copy ranges to exclude co-precompact extremely amenable subgroups of its actual pointwise group. The nonmetrisability implication remains external. [Native exclusion](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaLimitExtremelyAmenableDegree.lean) [Direct degree bound](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/FirstOrderExtremelyAmenableDegree.lean) For the nonmetrisability conclusion alone, the forward implication in Zucker 8.7 and the infinite copy degree also suffice by contraposition. [Fixed-point colouring argument](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/FixedPointColouring.lean) · [BMT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01068) · [Zucker](https://math.cmu.edu/~andrewz/GPP.pdf) |
| Covering criterion for co-precompact subgroups | Melleray–Nguyen Van Thé–Tsankov gives the finite UFH covering formulation for the right uniformity. The new double-coset lemma proves the algebraic equivalence of a finite cover at a point stabiliser and finitely many subgroup-orbits. Lean also proves the neighbourhood basis of finite tuple stabilisers, the equivalence with finite splitting of tuple orbits, and the pointwise tuple-agreement characterisation of closure. These criteria hold for the native BANANA group acting on its tagged two-sort tuples as well. [Native tuple criteria](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaLimitOrbitCoPrecompact.lean) Lean names the finite tuple orbits as relations in a canonical first-order expansion. The reduct automorphism group is the subgroup closure and has the same finite tuple orbits as the original subgroup. For a co-precompact subgroup of an oligomorphic group, Lean proves countable categoricity of this expansion. The native BANANA automorphism group is continuously isomorphic to that of the tagged presentation, so the subgroup closure and the categorical expansion transport to this presentation. [Native and tagged groups](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaLimitFirstOrderAutomorphism.lean) The converse implication from categoricity to oligomorphicity remains external. [Canonical orbit expansions](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/FirstOrderOrbitExpansion.lean) [Pointwise orbit criteria](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/FirstOrderOrbitCoPrecompact.lean) [Double-coset bridge](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/OrbitCover.lean) |
| Universal minimal flows and Polish automorphism groups | The classical existence and uniqueness of universal minimal flows supply the background to BMT and Zucker. Lean proves that the native BANANA automorphism group in its pointwise topology is Polish by embedding it as a closed subspace of the product of its forward and inverse point evaluations. [Native Polish group](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaLimitPolish.lean) |
| Amenability of dense unions of finite groups | Needed for the BANANA automorphism group and the groups preserving positive masses. The algebraic approximation is already checked for BANANA. Lean proves the general implication for a dense directed union of finite subgroups. The proof uses finite orbit averages, weak compactness and Riesz–Markov–Kakutani regularisation to obtain an invariant regular probability on every nonempty compact Hausdorff flow. In the concrete BANANA group, the finite GL stages give an increasing sequence with dense union for the pointwise topology. Applying the general theorem gives amenability of the full group. [Finite-subgroup amenability](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/FiniteSubgroupAmenability.lean) · [BANANA automorphism group](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/BananaLimitTopologicalAmenability.lean) |
| Amenability passes to open subgroups | Used for the Folkman stabiliser. The coinduction proof cited in the article is stated for extreme amenability. For amenability it uses the same compact coinduced space and pushes an invariant probability forward by evaluation at the identity. The amenability variant is now proved in Lean, by coinducing the compact flow and proving joint continuity using openness of the subgroup. [Open subgroup inheritance](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/OpenSubgroupAmenability.lean) |
| Stone representation and extension of a clopen charge | Used for nonamenability and for the positive-mass subgroups. Mathlib identifies idempotents with clopens in a prime spectrum. On a Boolean ring, every element is idempotent. The new Boolean spectrum module gives a compact Hausdorff space with all clopens represented by Boolean elements, and verifies equivariance under Boolean isomorphisms. It proves joint continuity from open inverse-evaluation fibres. Lean instantiates the continuous Stone action for the Boolean–BANANA limit and rules out an invariant probability. It also proves the native Folkman–BANANA nonamenability result using the open interval stabiliser, a continuous action on its Stone space and the actual two-sort pointwise group. [Folkman nonamenability](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/FolkmanFirstOrderLimitNonamenability.lean) The generic uniform-mass partition argument now forces the clopen values of every invariant probability, giving uniqueness on the countable Boolean algebra, maximality among amenable supergroups, failure of extreme amenability, and conjugacy invariance of the clopen mass range. Lean proves that an exhaustive coherent uniform tower supplies the uniform cover and a dense increasing union of finite subgroups, hence amenability. The concrete cylinder tower realises the weighted age and finite extension property. Marked back-and-forth identifies the reduct with the fixed Boolean–BANANA limit, on which Lean proves the unique invariant regular probability, the maximal amenable subgroup properties and the continuum conjugacy count. [Clopen probability extension](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/ClopenProbabilityExtension.lean) [Boolean–BANANA nonamenability](../BANANA/NonPrecompact/BooleanBananaStoneNonamenability.lean). | Compactness and Baire category for two-adic charges | Mathlib has compact p-adic integers, reductions to prime-power residue rings, the inverse-limit lift, and the Baire property of compact Hausdorff spaces. Lean constructs the closed charge space with the continuous action of the actual marked automorphism group, proves the fibre perturbation and meagreness result, and deduces orbit density given finite residue realisation for that action. Lean proves that the finite residue partition construction, all-marked finite ambients and finite marked homogeneity give dense and meagre orbits. For the concrete cylinder limit, Lean proves nonemptiness and minimality of the charge flow, with every orbit dense and meagre. This flow witnesses failure of the generic point property.
